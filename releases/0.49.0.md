# 0.49.0 — A task the system can stop, and an ABI that promises only what it does

**Tagged:** 2026-11-26. **Workspace version:** 0.49.0.
**RFCs:** RFC-0.49-001, RFC-0.49-002, RFC-0.49-003 (all `Implemented (0.49.0)`).
**CHANGELOG:** `## [0.49.0]`. **Reviewed over four stages**, with the records in `.git-exclude/reviewed/`.

---

## What this release is

Until this release **a task could be terminated only by itself**, and every task that stopped cost a
slot that never came back. There was no kill path in the kernel at all; `TaskKill` was a declared
`SyscallNumber` variant with no dispatch arm; and a service that hung — neither exiting nor faulting —
held its slot, its address space and all its authority for as long as the node ran, with no capability
anywhere able to stop it. 0.48 had made a privilege *withdrawable* from such a task. It had not made
the task *stoppable*.

`MAX_TASKS` is 40 and a measured boot occupies 32, so a node had **eight spawns for its entire
uptime**. A README that promises *"every documented failure mode has a tested playbook"* offered, for
a hung service, a power cycle.

Both halves are now closed, and one is deliberately not: **0.49 delivers reclamation, not restart.**

The second line made the ABI stop advertising what the kernel cannot do — `MmioUnmap` dispatched,
`DmaShare` retired, and `syscall-surface` turned from a record into a rule.

## What shipped

### RFC-0.49-001 — a task the system can stop, and a slot it can take back

`TaskKill` (43) dispatched and meaning **destroy**: a live task gets the death path it never had, its
endpoint/reply-edge/audit-ring/IRQ/ready-queue entries are purged, its CSpace is reset with every
slot's generation bumped, its user frames and kernel stack are freed, and the slot's generation
advances. An already-dead task has only its slot released, **so its verdict stays readable until a
`TASK_KILL` holder destroys it**, and nothing that reads a dead task had to change. `service-manager`
is the destroyer and reads a faulted task's verdict **before** releasing the slot.

Identity is real rather than conventional: **20 production `TaskId::new(_, 0)` sites → 0**, five
remaining in four classified functions, so a new occupant of a slot cannot inherit its predecessor's
leases, be woken by a stale id, or receive a reply meant for the dead. `bootstrap-leases` premise 6
retired in the same commit that falsified it, with its successor live and its count produced by the
gate rather than written by hand.

**Boot grants stopped discarding refusals.** The 55 `let _ = cs.install_raw(..)` sites became
`cs.grant(..)`, and `finish_grants()` fails the spawn on a refused grant **or a leftover capability**
— so reuse safety is an invariant checked at every spawn, which is more than the review asked for.

**Measured, not assumed:** reclaiming slots without frames would have moved the ceiling from 8 to
≈ 655–1,257 spawns and converted `TaskTableFull` into `NoMemory`, starving every other allocator
caller — which is why destroy frees frames. 18 of 24 frames are returned per destroy and **6
page-table frames are retained, measured** (the figure was derived by subtraction at stage 1 and is
now counted), giving ≈ 5,000 destroy-and-respawn cycles per boot. E-110's question — *is recycling
enough?* — was answered by measurement rather than by analogy: there is no restart loop to exhaust
anything, `service-manager` never spawns, `TaskCreate` is held by two tasks, so **no quota**, with the
condition that reopens it written into the RFC.

### RFC-0.49-002 — the ABI declares only syscalls the kernel dispatches

`sys_mmio_map` reserves its record **before any page-table entry**; `sys_mmio_unmap` is
capability-checked through the funnel, owner-only, whole-mapping and two-phase verified. **The device
page is never freed, and that is observed rather than asserted**: the kernel logs the allocator's free
count on both sides of every unmap and the profile requires the absence of any change. Death releases
the records. `bootstrap-leases` premise 5 retired and was replaced by a classification of all **15**
call sites of a device-unmap primitive, with lease revocation deliberately not among them — shown red
by putting one there.

`syscall-surface` is now **a rule rather than a record**: the `undispatched` key is retired, the
parser refuses it, and a new declared-but-undispatched variant fails the gate instead of being counted
by it.

**`DmaShare` (111) was retired on the owner's ruling**, not implemented: it routed *inter-service*
transfer through *device DMA* memory, and building it would have added a cross-task shared mapping — a
third holding the system cannot take back, held by two tasks — to the release that spent two
milestones proving it can take things back. Its number joins `120` in the ABI's retired-numbers note.

**The requirement it named stays open, as limitations row 28.** The implementer proposed wording
calling the chunked alternative *"a stated v1 limit, **not a pending feature**"*; **the architect
rejected that phrase and the owner agreed**, because this project's own documentation calls that path
*"a demonstration-scale workaround, not a designed transport"* and promoting it to the architecture
would be a workaround kept in the design. Its cost is now measured and pinned by a test — 4624 wire
bytes for the widest envelope, **147 round trips**, **294** for a full emission-and-forward path,
stated as upper bounds.

## What the architect got wrong, and who caught it

**Five errors of mine bear on this milestone. The implementer caught three; I found the fourth by
computing instead of reasoning, and the fifth by reading the last release's record against the tree.**

1. **"`TASK_KILL` exists and is granted by nothing today" — false.** `TASK_KILL` is bit 15 and
   `ALL_NON_META` is `(1 << 26) - 1`, so all three `TaskControl` grants already conferred it.
   **Dispatching `TaskKill` on my handoff's instruction would have armed a test fixture to destroy
   `init`.** The fourth time in three milestones I asserted an absence from a by-name search that a
   blanket constant defeated — and the reason `capability-topology` now prints the blanket's whole
   expansion rather than counting the one right.
2. **"Decide the kill holder as RFC-0.46-003 decided the lease revoker" — wrong, measurably.** `init`
   exits in every boot, so an owner-only rule would let no one kill any service. The same fact makes
   **every** boot lease 0.48 creates owned by a dead task; my 0.48 review had called that a corner
   case, and it is the normal case. Limitations row 12 now says so, and names the single holder that
   can withdraw a boot capability: `neg-test`.
3. **"A public item disappears from `abi-snapshot`" — wrong.** A variant is not an item; the count
   stays and the enum's hash moves. The implementer measured it and diffed `--dump-members` against
   the base rather than inferring it.
4. **`releases/0.48.0.md`'s "`git diff 0.48.0 HEAD` is empty" was false when I wrote it** — E-136,
   found at this cut by reading the previous record against the tree. A release record is a document
   the architect alone writes, and this is the second of mine to carry a false claim about itself or
   its subject.
5. **I nearly published a bulk-transfer figure an order of magnitude wrong**, reading a `n < 400`
   assertion that covers one payload variant as the envelope's size. `MAX_WIRE_BYTES` is 4624. I
   computed it before recommending, and the recommendation would otherwise have been the opposite.
   **The implementer then qualified my own 147/294 as upper bounds**, which flat would have overstated
   the typical cost — the same error as the figures they replaced.

**Also recorded, because this release's own verification found them**: the capacity figure for the
mapping table was sized from call sites rather than live instances (16 → 64, caught by two tiers going
red, not by review); and `ReadyQueue::remove` left a bucket's bit set in `non_empty_mask` and **hung
the machine**, found by booting.

## Three errata filed, one closed on filing

**E-134** — `fjell-kernel` carries **27 `#[test]` functions that cannot compile**. `cargo test -p
fjell-kernel` fails with 19 errors, so not one has ever run: not skipped, not ignored, counted by
nothing. Found by the scheduler bug above, in a file holding three of them. **Independently
demonstrated at this cut**: counting the host bin tests by hand fails on exactly this, which is why
the suite needs an exclude list. No published figure was inflated, so nothing drifted — what misleads
is the source tree. **ACCEPTED**, row 29.

**E-135 — found during this cut, not during the milestone.** Writing RFC-0.49-001's `Evidence:` line I
went to cite `marker:task-lifecycle:…` and there was nothing to resolve against: the two new profiles
have no recorded marker artefacts, and neither has `fleet-demo` in any commit. The cause is not a
missed `git add` — `write_summary` writes those files **only when they are already on disk**, a
deliberate fix so *ungated* profiles do not leak untracked files, which also means a newly gated
profile is absent, therefore never written, therefore never committed. **Three of 27 gated profiles**,
and nothing compares the gated set against the recorded set. Every gated tier's markers *are* still
checked, because the profile's own TOML is the authority. **ACCEPTED**, row 30.

**E-136 — the architect's own, and about the previous release's record.** `releases/0.48.0.md` offers
*"`git diff 0.48.0 HEAD` is **empty**"* as its proof that E-130's reorder worked, and that sentence is
**one of the sixty-nine lines that made the diff non-empty**: it was added by `b2c17b7`, the follow-up
commit appending that record's tag-and-publish section, and `git diff 0.48.0 b2c17b7` reports 69
insertions and 1 deletion in that very file. Verified by finding the sentence among that commit's own
added lines, not inferred. **It was false when written, not made false later.** What it was reaching
for is true: no source, gate input, instrument output or build artefact differs between the tag and the
commit after it — only the record's own account of the tag, which cannot exist before the tag it
describes. **Released records are not rewritten** (the rule that governs E-126 and E-133), so the claim
stands in 0.48.0 for ever and this record states the property in the form that holds — see *Tag and
publish*. **No gate could have caught it**: `consistency-check` is by design a pure function of the
committed tree, and a tag is a ref. That boundary is correct, and it leaves this class to a reader.
**CLOSED** on filing, as a record rather than as a correction.

**Neither of the two ACCEPTED errata was hand-seeded or patched at this cut, deliberately.** Seeding E-135's files would have made
the register consistent while hiding the defect that produced it, and both fixes are code — which does
not belong in a release commit after the verification run. The two RFC Evidence lines cite the profile
and its code instead of a marker, which is the honest citation and is why the gap was found at all.

## Tag and publish

**This section is written in two commits, and says so rather than claiming otherwise.** The release
commit carries everything a tag can point at; the tag's own identity, the CI run for that exact SHA,
the GitHub Release and the crate publishes can only be measured afterwards, so they are appended by a
follow-up commit. **That is the two-commit shape E-130's reorder leaves, and E-136 is the erratum for
describing it as an empty diff.**

**What is true of the tag, and is the property the reorder bought:** no source file, gate input,
instrument output or build artefact differs between the tagged commit and the commit that follows it.
The only difference is this section's measured rows, which cannot exist before the tag they describe.
That is checkable — `git diff <tag> HEAD --stat` names `releases/0.49.0.md` and nothing else — and it
is what *"tree, tag and publish are one commit"* means. It does not mean the diff is empty.

**Before the tag:** the pre-publish landing-page re-read runs first, by E-130's reorder, so any
correction it finds lands *in* the tagged commit rather than after it. `release-preflight 0.49.0`
judges what a tag makes checkable — bare tag name, workspace version at that commit, ancestor of
`main`, a CHANGELOG section, a release record — and the publish precondition requires `HEAD` to be the
tag's commit with a clean tree.

## Known limitations at this release

No hardware has ever booted this system. There is no working network plane. Three of the four
measurement-chain events are placeholders. **A reclaimed slot is not a restarted service** — nothing
refills one, `service-manager` holds no `TaskCreate`, and granting it one would make the third holder
under which a quota becomes necessary. Page-table frames are not freed, bounding a boot at ≈ 5,000
destroy-and-respawn cycles. **Lease revocation reclaims no resource** — not a DMA frame, not an MMIO
mapping — and whether it should is stated as an open question rather than answered by implementation.
There is no designed bulk transport. The kernel crate has no runnable unit tests. Three gated boot
profiles have no recorded marker artefacts. Documentation deployment fails intermittently on GitHub's
side with no mechanism established, and a cut may need a job re-run.

**One mutation result in this milestone is derived rather than observed**, and the record says so: the
`mmio` profile lost five markers under K14 and the implementer named four, so the fifth —
`UNMAP_TWICE_REFUSED` — is the architect's elimination from the eight the profile carries, not a
reading.

**Two mutations were GREEN and are recorded as information, not as passes:** the explicit endpoint
purge and the reply-edge purge are reached by no boot scenario (the boot-lease revocation removes a
lease-bound waiter first, and no fixture leaves a reply edge naming a destroyed task). Host tests in
`fjell-ipc` carry them; **the boot's coverage of those two paths is nil.**

**No accepted-risk statement is written for this release.** No gate that should have blocked was left
unpassed; the one red instrument this cut produced was a stale repro baseline, and it was re-recorded
rather than accepted.
